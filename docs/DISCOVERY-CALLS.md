# Discovery calls

A fifteen-minute call with one member of staff about their recent work. The calls run before any tool is suggested, and they produce the evidence that this site's guides, case studies and assessment questions are built on. This document is the guide for running one.

It is a working method from one practitioner's practice, not a standard. Change it when the evidence says to, and record the change in the pull request.

## Purpose

Three things, in this order:

1. Find what is slow or painful for this person right now, in their own words.
2. Find what they have already tried, so the first intervention does not repeat it.
3. Produce one coded row for [the evidence sheet](EVIDENCE-CODING.md).

A discovery call is not a sales call, not a training session and not a demonstration. If the caller opens a tool during the call, the call has failed.

## Before the call

- Fifteen minutes, booked, one person. Not a group.
- Ask for team leads and staff in roughly equal numbers. The two groups name different pains.
- Read nothing about AI beforehand. Read what the person does.

## The consent line

Say this at the start, in your own words, before any question:

> This is a fifteen-minute conversation about your recent work. I take notes. Nothing you say is attributed to you by name without your written approval. If the notes feed a public guide or case study, the organisation can be anonymised and you will see the exact text first. Is that all right?

Wait for a yes. Write the date of the yes on the note.

## The question bank

Ask in this order. Follow what the person says; do not read the list aloud. Every question is about work that has already happened. None names a tool.

**Recent work**

- What did you work on this week?
- Walk me through one of those. Where did the time go?
- What did you finish? What is still open?

**Bottlenecks**

- Where did you wait for something or someone?
- What did you do twice?
- What did you do by hand that felt like it should not be by hand?

**What they dislike**

- What part of the week did you put off?
- If one task disappeared from your job, which one?
- What do you do that nobody would notice if it stopped?

**What they already tried**

- Have you tried anything to make that faster? What happened?
- Is there something you set up once and stopped using? Why?
- Who do you ask when you are stuck on a tool?

**Close**

- Is there anything I should have asked?

## Rules

- No leading questions. "Do you use AI for that?" is leading. "How do you do that?" is not.
- No tool pitched. Not even in the close.
- If the person asks "what could AI do for this?", write the question down and say you will come back to it after the calls are grouped.
- Let silence run. The second answer is usually the real one.
- Fifteen minutes means fifteen minutes. Stop on time and offer a second call if there is more.

## Note template

One note per call, in plain text. Keep the first four lines fixed so the notes can be coded without re-reading.

```
Date:            2026-09-05
Role type:       team lead | staff | leadership
Consent:         yes, 2026-09-05
Anonymise org:   yes | no

Recent work:
Bottlenecks:
Dislikes:
Already tried:
Asked about AI:  yes | no  (what they asked)
Quote (with permission to use, anonymised):
One-line summary of the pain:
```

Notes are private working material. They never enter this public repository. Only the coded row and an approved quote do.

## After the call

Within a day, while the call is fresh:

1. Write the one-line summary of the pain, in the person's words where possible.
2. Add one row to the evidence sheet, using the controlled codes in [EVIDENCE-CODING.md](EVIDENCE-CODING.md).
3. Do not propose an intervention yet. Interventions come from the grouped rows, not from one call.

When the calls for one organisation are done, group the rows by need and by blocker. The largest group is the first intervention. Show the grouped map to the whole organisation before building anything.

## Closing the loop

Every person who gave a call gets a short message when the grouped map exists, saying what was heard and what will happen first. This is the difference between research and extraction.
